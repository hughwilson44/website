abstract: Single-molecule fluorescence-based techniques have emerged to provide powerful and versatile means of sensing nanoscale structure and dynamics. In aqueous solution, molecular diffusion limits the typical observation time of a single molecule to ~1 millisecond, posing a bottleneck to the amount of information that can be extracted. To resolve this fundamental limitation, the Anti-Brownian ELectrokinetic (ABEL) trap was developed and enables long-term (~10 seconds), information-rich interrogation of single molecules in solution. In this chapter, we review the basic principles and technical development of the ABEL trap, as well as recent applications of the device to dissect dynamic processes at the nanoscale level. Finally, we provide a tutorial protocol that illustrates the procedures key to an ABEL trap experiment.
